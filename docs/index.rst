
How to Restart Your Router
===========================

Restarting a router is one of the simplest ways to troubleshoot common internet connection problems. A restart can help when websites are not loading, Wi-Fi becomes unstable, connected devices lose internet access, or the connection suddenly becomes slow.

A router restart temporarily turns the device off and then starts it again. This allows the router to establish a fresh connection with your internet service and can clear temporary network problems.

This guide explains how to restart a router safely, what to check before restarting, and what to do if your internet connection does not return afterward.

Why Restart Your Router?
-------------------------

Routers operate continuously and manage connections between your devices and the internet. Temporary software or connection problems can sometimes affect their performance.

Restarting the router can help resolve issues such as:

* Wi-Fi disconnecting frequently
* Internet pages failing to load
* Slow or unstable connections
* Devices being unable to connect
* Temporary network errors
* Problems after an internet service interruption

A restart does not normally change your saved Wi-Fi name, password, or configuration settings.

Check Your Internet Connection
------------------------------

Before restarting the router, check whether the problem affects all connected devices.

Try opening a website on another phone, computer, or tablet connected to the same network.

If only one device has a problem, restarting the router may not be necessary. The issue could be related to that individual device.

If multiple devices cannot access the internet, the router or internet connection may be responsible.

Save Your Online Work
---------------------

Before restarting the router, save anything important that you are doing online.

A restart temporarily disconnects every device connected through the router.

Online meetings, downloads, games, file transfers, and other activities may be interrupted.

Once the router reconnects, affected devices should normally be able to access the internet again.

Locate Your Router
------------------

Find the router provided by your internet service provider or the router you installed yourself.

A router normally has several indicator lights and network cables connected to it.

Some internet setups use a separate modem and router, while other setups combine both devices into one unit.

Identify the correct device before disconnecting any cables.

Turn Off the Router
-------------------

If your router has a power button, use it to turn the device off.

Wait for the router's lights to turn off.

If there is no power button, disconnect the router's power adapter from the electrical outlet.

Do not remove the Ethernet or other network cables unless necessary.

Wait Before Turning It Back On
-------------------------------

Leave the router powered off for a short period.

This allows temporary network processes to stop completely.

You do not normally need to leave the router disconnected for an extended period.

After waiting, reconnect the power adapter or turn the router back on.

Wait for the Router to Restart
-------------------------------

After restoring power, the router may take some time to initialize.

The indicator lights may change while the device establishes its connection.

Wait until the normal internet and Wi-Fi indicators appear.

Do not repeatedly turn the router off and on while it is starting.

Reconnect Your Devices
----------------------

Once the router has fully restarted, reconnect your phone, computer, tablet, smart television, or other devices if they do not reconnect automatically.

Open the Wi-Fi settings on your device.

Select your wireless network.

Enter the Wi-Fi password if requested.

Wait for the device to establish a connection.

Test Your Internet Connection
------------------------------

Open a browser and visit a few websites.

If webpages load normally, the router restart was successful.

You can also test multiple devices to confirm that the connection is working throughout your home or office.

Restart a Modem and Router
--------------------------

If your internet setup includes both a modem and a separate router, you may need to restart both devices.

Turn off the router and modem.

Wait briefly.

Restore power to the modem first.

Allow the modem to establish its connection.

Then turn the router back on.

Wait for both devices to finish starting.

Reconnect your devices and test the internet connection.

The exact startup process can vary depending on your internet service provider and equipment.

Router Restart vs Factory Reset
-------------------------------

A router restart is different from a factory reset.

A restart simply turns the router off and back on.

A factory reset removes the router's saved configuration and returns it to its default settings.

Do not press and hold the reset button unless you specifically intend to perform a factory reset.

After a factory reset, you may need to configure the Wi-Fi network, internet connection, security settings, and other options again.

If you only want to fix a temporary connection problem, use a normal restart instead.

Router Restart Did Not Fix the Internet
----------------------------------------

If restarting the router does not restore internet access, check the cables.

Make sure the power cable is securely connected.

Check the cable connecting the router to the modem or internet service equipment.

If you have a separate modem, check its status lights.

You should also check whether there is an outage affecting your internet service.

Try restarting the affected device as well.

If multiple devices remain offline, contact your internet service provider for additional assistance.

Check Wi-Fi Signal Strength
---------------------------

If the internet works near the router but becomes unreliable in another room, the issue may be related to Wi-Fi signal strength rather than the internet connection itself.

Move closer to the router and test the connection.

Large walls, floors, furniture, appliances, and other wireless networks can affect Wi-Fi performance.

If coverage is consistently poor, consider improving router placement or using an appropriate Wi-Fi extender or mesh system.

Update Router Firmware
----------------------

Router manufacturers may release firmware updates that improve security, stability, and compatibility.

Check your router's administration settings or manufacturer's instructions for available updates.

Only install firmware intended for your exact router model.

Avoid interrupting the router while a firmware update is being installed.

Keep Your Router Well Positioned
--------------------------------

Router placement can affect wireless performance.

Place the router in an open and reasonably central location when possible.

Avoid placing it inside cabinets or behind large objects.

Keep it away from sources of excessive heat.

A better location can improve wireless coverage and reduce connection problems.

When to Contact Your Internet Provider
--------------------------------------

Contact your internet service provider if the connection remains unavailable after restarting the equipment and checking the cables.

The problem may be caused by an outage, service issue, line problem, modem problem, or account-related issue.

Your provider can check the connection remotely and determine whether additional troubleshooting or equipment replacement is necessary.

Conclusion
----------

Knowing how to restart your router can help resolve many temporary internet and Wi-Fi problems.

Save your online work, turn off the router, disconnect its power when necessary, wait briefly, and restore power. Allow the router to finish starting before reconnecting and testing your devices.

If your setup includes a separate modem, you may need to restart both devices in the appropriate order.

Remember that restarting is not the same as performing a factory reset. Avoid using the router's reset button unless you intend to erase its configuration.

If restarting the router does not restore your connection, check the cables, test multiple devices, look for a possible service outage, and contact your internet provider when necessary.

Regular router restarts are not usually required, but knowing how to restart your router safely can be a useful troubleshooting skill when connection problems occur.
